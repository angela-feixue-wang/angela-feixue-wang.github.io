Organic solar cells (OPVs) are gaining interest as a solar cell technology due to their lightweight and low-temperature solution-based printing advantages over traditional silicon-based solar cells. However, OPV design optimisation often relies on trial and error, which is time-consuming and generates significant material wastage. In response, this paper demonstrates a streamlined computational simulation approach for the design optimisation of a novel OPV architecture, ITO/SnO\(_2\)/PTQ10:FCC-Cl/BM-HTL/Ag. The study is split into two stages. First, single cell layer thickness optimisation was performed using an opto-electrical model in OghmaNano. Then, the single cell was upscaled to a module, and module-level design optimisation was performed through a discretised electrical mesh method. The 0.15 cm\(^2\) single cell achieved a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 14.60% at optimised layer thicknesses: ITO 100 nm, SnO\(_2\) 50 nm, PTQ10:FCC-Cl 280 nm, BM-HTL 50 nm, and Ag 100 nm. Module design optimisation produced PCE heatmaps to provide practical fabrication guidance for layer thickness, layer offset, stripe width, and number of sub-cells. The optimised module had 100 cm\(^2\) module area, 5 sub-cells, PTQ10:FCC-Cl thickness 200 nm, and achieved a PCE of 7.25% comparable to current roll-to-roll slot-die coated module performances. The systematic optimisation approach offers practical design insights for scalable module production, reducing development times and material wastage in OPV development.
